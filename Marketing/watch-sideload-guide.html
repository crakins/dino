Dinosaur · Watch App

Getting the app onto an Apple Watch you don't own

Two ways to get a build onto someone else's watch. Sideload is faster and free, but runs through your Mac and their iPhone directly, cable in hand. TestFlight takes longer to set up and costs $99/yr, but once it's running, anyone can install over the air with just an email invite.

You don't need the watch, or the iPhone it's paired to, logged into your account. Code signing happens inside Xcode with an Apple ID you add there — the device itself only needs to trust your Mac and your developer certificate.

scheme: Dinosaur Watch App target: watchOS 11.4+ ~15 min, free Apple ID

The two accounts are unrelated. The Apple ID you sign into Xcode's Settings › Accounts generates the certificate the app is built with. The Apple ID on the watch's iPhone just needs the resulting app to be trusted — it never has to match, and it never has to sign in to anything.

01

Pair the watch to a nearby iPhone

You can't install straight onto a watch — Xcode always deploys through the iPhone it's paired with. Any iPhone works, signed into any account or none. Pair them the normal way if they aren't already (Watch app › Start Pairing), then connect that iPhone to your Mac with a cable, or over Wi-Fi once it shows up once via cable.

On the phone, tap Trust when your Mac prompts for it.

02

Add your own Apple ID to Xcode

In Xcode: Xcode › Settings › Accounts › + › sign in with any Apple ID you control. A free, non-paid Apple ID is enough — you get a "Personal Team," which is all sideloading needs.

This account signs the build. It has no relationship to whoever owns the watch or the iPhone.

03

Point signing at your Personal Team

Open Dinosaur.xcodeproj, then for both targets — Dinosaur and Dinosaur Watch App:

  • Select the target › Signing & Capabilities
  • Check Automatically manage signing
  • Set Team to the Personal Team from step 2
If signing fails with "no account for team": the bundle ID com.a6enterprises.Dinosaur.watchkitapp may already be claimed on this Apple ID by an earlier build. Append something unique, e.g. .dev, on both targets' bundle identifiers and re-run.
04

Select the watch as the run destination

Confirm the watch shows up in Window › Devices and Simulators once the iPhone is connected — watchOS devices appear automatically through their paired phone, no separate cable needed.

Then in the scheme bar, choose the Dinosaur Watch App scheme and pick the physical watch (not a simulator) as the destination.

05

Turn on Developer Mode

Both the iPhone and the watch need Developer Mode enabled once, the first time you sideload anything:

  • iPhone: Settings › Privacy & Security › Developer Mode › toggle on › restart
  • Watch: Settings › Privacy & Security › Developer Mode on the watch itself › toggle on › restart

If the toggle isn't visible yet, run step 6 once first — Xcode surfaces it as part of the install attempt.

06

Build, trust, run

Press ⌘R. The first install will likely stop with an untrusted-developer warning. Clear it on the iPhone:

  • Settings › General › VPN & Device Management
  • Tap your Personal Team's profile under "Developer App"
  • Tap Trust

Run again. It launches straight on the watch face.

Free-account limits

The app disappears after a week

Personal Team certificates expire every 7 days. Reopen the project in Xcode and hit ⌘R again to re-sign and refresh it — no data is lost on the watch.

Xcode says you've hit an app limit

A free Apple ID can have at most 3 apps sideloaded at once across your devices. Remove an old one from the watch (press and hold the icon › Remove App) to free a slot.

Do I need the watch's iCloud password?

No. Nothing in this flow asks for it. The only credential involved is the Apple ID you added to Xcode in step 2, and it only ever touches your Mac.

TestFlight skips Xcode's device-trust dance entirely — you build once, upload it to App Store Connect, then whoever's watch it is installs it through the TestFlight app using their own Apple ID. Unlike sideloading, that Apple ID does need to accept an invite — it just doesn't need to be yours, or the one Xcode is signed into.

scheme: Dinosaur requires: paid Apple Developer Program ~30 min + review wait

One requirement sideloading doesn't have: TestFlight only exists for apps built under a paid Apple Developer Program membership ($99/yr) — a free Personal Team can sign builds locally but can't upload to App Store Connect. If step 1 below is already true for you, skip to step 2.

01

Enroll in the Apple Developer Program

Under your own Apple ID, at developer.apple.com/programs › Enroll. This is the account that owns the app record and the TestFlight build — it's separate from whichever Apple ID ends up testing on the watch.

In Xcode, add it under Xcode › Settings › Accounts if it isn't there yet.

02

Register the app in App Store Connect

At appstoreconnect.apple.com › Apps › + › New App:

  • Platform: watchOS (App Store Connect adds iOS automatically for the companion container)
  • Bundle ID: com.a6enterprises.Dinosaur.watchkitapp — register it under Certificates, Identifiers & Profiles first if it doesn't appear in the picker
  • SKU: anything unique, e.g. dinosaur-watch-tf
03

Set signing to your team, then archive

Open Dinosaur.xcodeproj. For both the Dinosaur and Dinosaur Watch App targets, set Signing & Capabilities › Team to your Developer Program team, automatic signing on.

Select the Dinosaur scheme (the container — archiving the watch app scheme directly isn't offered), destination Any watchOS Device, then Product › Archive.

"Archive" is greyed out: that means a Simulator destination is still selected. Archive only appears once a real device or "Any watchOS Device" is chosen.
04

Upload from the Organizer

When the archive finishes, the Organizer window opens automatically › select it › Distribute App › TestFlight & App Store › Upload, keeping the default automatic signing options.

Processing on Apple's side typically takes 10–30 minutes. You'll get an email once the build is ready to test.

05

Clear Export Compliance

In App Store Connect › TestFlight, the new build shows a missing-compliance warning until answered. This app doesn't add its own encryption, so answer No to "Does your app use encryption" — the build then moves to Ready to Test.

06

Invite the watch's Apple ID as a tester

Still in TestFlight › Internal Testing › create a group › add the build, then add a tester by the email address signed into that iPhone (the one the target watch is paired to). Internal testers install instantly, no review wait — up to 100 people, and they need to be added to your team in App Store Connect › Users and Access first.

Testing someone else's watch, no App Store Connect access to add them? Use External Testing instead — add by email without a team invite. It requires a one-time Apple Beta App Review (usually under a day) before the link works.
07

Install on the phone, then the watch

On the paired iPhone, signed into the invited Apple ID:

  • Accept the TestFlight email invite (or open the public link for external testing)
  • Install the TestFlight app from the App Store if it isn't already there
  • Open the invite in TestFlight › Install

The watchOS companion installs on the paired watch on its own within a minute or two — no separate step on the watch itself.

Common snags

The watch app never shows up after the phone install

Open the Watch app on the iPhone › scroll to the Dinosaur entry under "Available Apps" and tap Install — some watchOS versions don't auto-push a first install.

Builds expire

TestFlight builds stop working 90 days after upload. Archive and upload again from Xcode to refresh — testers get an update prompt, no re-invite needed.

Do I need the watch owner's Apple ID password?

No — you only need their email to send the invite. They accept it and install using their own account, on their own device, without sharing credentials with you.

"No suitable application records were found" on upload

The bundle ID in Xcode doesn't match one registered in App Store Connect yet. Revisit step 2 and confirm the watch app's bundle ID was registered before creating the app record.
